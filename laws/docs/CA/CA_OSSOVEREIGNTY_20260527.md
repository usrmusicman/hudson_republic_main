## Definitions

- **Free and Open Source Software (FOSS)**: Software whose source code is publicly available, allowing inspection, modification, and redistribution by the community.  
- **Open Hardware**: Physical systems designed with publicly available schematics, firmware, and documentation, enabling full auditing and modification.  
- **Right to Repair / Right to Refurbish**: The right of owners to maintain, repair, and modify legally owned hardware and software without artificial restrictions.  
- **Generally Available Parts**: Replacement components that are openly available for purchase by the general public, independent repair shops, or end-users without requiring authorization, registration, or special agreements with the manufacturer.

## Preamble

The Hudson Republic recognizes that genuine sovereignty requires technological transparency and independence. Reliance on closed-source (“black box”) systems creates serious risks of hidden vulnerabilities, external control, and loss of accountability. This Act establishes a clear preference for Free and Open Source Software and Open Hardware across all government operations while allowing narrowly defined exceptions for the most sensitive national security requirements.

---

## Section 1 — FOSS Mandate

**1.1** Free and Open Source Software shall be the preferred and primary standard for all governance, administrative, and non-sensitive infrastructure systems.  
**1.2** All government entities shall prioritize FOSS solutions to ensure transparency, security, auditability, and adaptability.

---

## Section 2 — Closed Source Software

**2.1** Closed-source software shall be used only as a last resort and only when no suitable FOSS alternative exists.  
**2.2** Any approved use of closed-source software must be accompanied by a funded, time-bound migration plan to transition to FOSS alternatives.  
**2.3** Funding for closed-source solutions shall be treated as temporary migration budgets and shall automatically cease upon completion of the transition period.

---

## Section 3 — Open Hardware Mandate

**3.1** Open Hardware shall be strongly preferred for all government-procured computing, networking, surveillance, and critical infrastructure systems.  
**3.2** Open Hardware enables full auditing of hardware and firmware, thereby protecting the Republic’s security and sovereignty.  
**3.3** The Republic shall support community-driven development of Open Hardware to reduce foreign supply chain dependency.

---

## Section 4 — Closed Hardware

**4.1** Closed hardware may only be used when no suitable open alternative exists.  
**4.2** Such hardware must include verifiable circuit-level kill switches or disabling mechanisms for sensitive components (microphones, cameras, wireless radios, etc.).  
**4.3** All closed hardware must undergo rigorous independent security audits before deployment.

---

## Section 5 — National Security Exception

**5.1** Notwithstanding the above, limited exceptions may be granted for highly sensitive top-secret military, intelligence, or cryptographic systems where disclosure of technical details would create an unacceptable risk to national security.  
**5.2** Any such exception must be approved by an **Overwhelming Majority** verdict as defined in the **[Clarity Act](./CA_CLARITYACT_20260522.md)** and shall be subject to regular review.  
**5.3** This exception shall be interpreted narrowly and shall not be used to circumvent the general principles of this Act.

---

## Section 6 — Open Innovation Funding

**6.1** All public funding for defense, infrastructure, and administrative software or hardware development shall be granted exclusively to projects released under approved FOSS or Open Hardware licenses, except where the national security exception in Section 5 applies.

---

## Section 7 — Right to Repair and Right to Refurbish

**7.1** All hardware and software purchased or owned by the Republic, its Ridings, or citizens shall respect the Right to Repair and Right to Refurbish.  
**7.2** Replacement parts must be **generally available** to the public, independent repair shops, and end-users without requiring manufacturer authorization, registration, or special agreements.  
**7.3** Prohibited practices include timed obsolescence, self-destruction mechanisms, DRM that prevents full offline functionality, locked bootloaders without unlock keys, and unnecessary soldered components that impede repair.  
**7.4** Replacement parts must be made available for a minimum of ten (10) years after product release, unless the manufacturer ceases operations.  
**7.5** The Republic is committed to reducing electronic waste through repair, refurbishment, and responsible recycling.

---

## Section EX1 — Illustrative Examples (Non-binding)

**EX1.1** A new document management system for civilian administration must use FOSS unless a compelling case is made and a migration plan is approved.  

**EX1.2** Specialized military communication devices may qualify for the national security exception in Section 5, but standard administrative computers must use Open Hardware where possible.  

**EX1.3** A citizen purchases a smartphone. The device must support bootloader unlocking, generally available replacement parts, and full offline functionality after purchase.
