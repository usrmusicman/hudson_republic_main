## Definitions
- **XRF Assay**: Non-destructive X-ray fluorescence analysis used to verify elemental composition and purity of bullion.  
- **Fundamental Parameters (FP) Method**: Physics-based calibration method that accounts for matrix effects in variable alloys.  
- **Certified Reference Material (CRM)**: Standardized sample with known elemental composition used for calibration and verification.  
- **Specific Gravity Testing**: Density measurement used to detect high-density fillers such as tungsten cores.

---

## Preamble
This Code establishes mandatory technical standards and procedures for XRF verification of bullion. It ensures the integrity of the Hudson Republic’s tri-metal monetary system and supports the Bring Your Own Bullion (BYOB) program through rigorous calibration, open sovereignty requirements, and multi-layered verification protocols.

---

## Section 1 — XRF Verification Mandate
**1.1** All bullion used as legal tender or held in reserves must undergo XRF assay to verify compliance with purity standards in the **[Bullion Standards and Conversion Act](../LA/LA_BULLIONSTANDARDS_20260401.md)**.  
**1.2** XRF verification takes precedence over weight-based measurements when discrepancies arise.

---

## Section 2 — Calibration and Tolerance Limits
**2.1** All XRF terminals must undergo Energy Calibration and Quantitative Calibration using the Fundamental Parameters (FP) Method.  
**2.2** Calibration shall use solid Certified Reference Materials (CRMs) representing the 95% commercial and 99.5% institutional purity thresholds.  
**2.3** Terminals must return readings within the following margins of error:  
- **Commercial Coins/Rounds (95% target)**: ±0.3%  
- **Institutional Bars (99.5% target)**: ±0.1%

---

## Section 3 — Non-Destructive Testing for High-Density Fillers
**3.1** Because XRF is a surface analysis technique, supplementary non-destructive methods are required to detect high-density fillers such as tungsten cores.  
**3.2** Approved supplementary tests include specific gravity (density) measurement, ultrasonic thickness testing, and electrical conductivity testing.  
**3.3** Items failing supplementary tests shall be rejected or referred for laboratory analysis.

---

## Section 4 — Open Sovereignty Requirements
**4.1** All XRF terminals must export raw spectral data in an open, non-proprietary format.  
**4.2** Proprietary black-box algorithms that prevent data export or independent verification are prohibited for MTI/FHI-certified terminals.  
**4.3** Daily drift checks using baseline CRMs are mandatory. Significant drift requires full re-calibration.

---

## Section 5 — Supplementary Merchant Screening (Low-Value Transactions)
**5.1** For small commercial and peer-to-peer transactions, merchants may use a preliminary magnetic screening for Hudson copper bullion (HCB1 and HCB8).  
**5.2** Genuine Hudson copper/iron alloy bullion exhibits slight magnetic attraction when tested with a strong neodymium magnet. Complete non-reactivity or overly strong attraction indicates a potentially non-compliant item.  
**5.3** This magnetic test serves as a quick preliminary screen only and does not replace formal XRF verification.

---

## Section 6 — Compliance and Auditing
**6.1** FHI-certified inspectors shall conduct periodic audits, including blind CRM testing.  
**6.2** Failure to maintain required calibration standards, use of prohibited equipment, or falsification of results constitutes a material breach under the **[Banking and Reserves Act](../CA/CA_BANKANDRESERVE_20260401.md)**, resulting in revocation of MTI/FHI coverage and applicable penalties.
